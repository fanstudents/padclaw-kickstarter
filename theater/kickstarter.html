The problem

Who answers your mother's questions?

We spent a year asking American families that question. The answer was never a device. It was always a person: a daughter in another city reading the weather over the phone, a grandson resetting a router on a video call, a neighbor writing the Wi-Fi password on a sticky note.

Every "smart" screen we tested assumed things Barbara doesn't have: patience for menus, eyesight for 12-point type, and an account for everything. The devices weren't dumb. They were built for someone else.

214US households interviewed between March 2025 and February 2026, ages 61 to 84, together with their adult children.
83%of the "tech support" calls families described were questions a single spoken sentence could have answered.
40 minmedian time per week adult children spent as phone-based tech support for a parent. Every week.

Why we built it

Barbara owns a smartphone. She calls Emily instead.

Emily set up her mother's phone the right way. Big icons, emergency contacts, a weather app on the first screen. Barbara still calls her at 7:40 most mornings, because the app answers with radar maps and an umbrella icon, and Emily answers with "wear the light jacket, Mom."

That call is the entire product brief. A machine that answers the way Emily does: one sentence, about your actual day, in a voice that isn't in a hurry.

"I don't need another app. I need something that just works without me having to think about it."

How you use it

Say it out loud. That's the whole manual.

Four far-field microphones pick up a conversational voice from about four meters, over a running faucet. Ask "what should I wear today?" from the stove and it answers in eight words. The screen prints what it heard in 24-point type, so you know it got it right.

There is one design rule in the entire operating system: every screen has exactly one thing to do next. No second-level menus exist. Not one.

And when the answer is delivered, PadClaw does something no other screen in your house does. It stops. No feed, no autoplay, no red badge asking to be tapped.

The brain / hands split

The AI plans the work.
It never holds your keys.

Most assistants have one soul. PadClaw has two, on purpose, and they are not allowed to merge.

🧠 The brain — decides

Understands the request and plans the steps. Everyday requests run on the device itself. Harder ones run on our own servers in Oregon. Sensitive topics never leave that boundary.

  • Roughly 4 of 5 requests are handled on-device
  • Health, money and family messages stay on PadClaw infrastructure
  • Optional: plug in your own OpenAI, Gemini or Anthropic key

🤝 The hands — act, with permission

Logins, card numbers and files live in a dedicated secure element on the device. The brain can request an action; only the hands can perform it, and only after you approve it on screen, written in plain words.

  • Every outward action stops at an approval gate first
  • A 90-day log of every action, kept on the device
  • One button erases everything, device and servers both

Boring on purpose. Screwed, not glued.

Risks & challenges

The honest part

Certification timing

FCC and UL testing can add four to six weeks when a revision is required. We start testing during the campaign on pre-screened units, and the February date already includes one re-test cycle. If we clear on the first pass, units ship early. We won't promise that; it has happened to us once.

Component supply

The display is a mature 12.4" panel available from two suppliers; the SoC platform has been in volume production for over two years. We deliberately chose no custom-cut, single-source parts. Battery cells carry UN38.3 certification for air freight.

Software scope

Everything you clicked in the demo is planned software: the design is final, the voice pipeline works, and the data is sample data. If a channel isn't ready in February, the device still ships on time and the channel arrives over the air. Hardware waits for nothing.

The uncomfortable one

We're a small company. If PadClaw the service ever shut down, the device would keep working: on-device speech, reminders, check-ins and the Android tablet underneath don't depend on our servers. We designed for our own absence. We'd rather tell you that now than have you wonder.

FAQ

Asked constantly, answered plainly

What happens when the six free months end?

The device keeps working, full stop. On-device AI — speech, reminders, check-ins, timers — stays unlimited and free forever. The optional Family plan ($14/month, price locked for founders) adds private-cloud requests for long reasoning, chart math and translation. We ask you 14 days before the free period ends. Nothing renews by itself.

Is my family's data used to train AI models?

No. Speech is transcribed on the device, sensitive requests stay on our own servers in Oregon, nothing is sold or shared, and a 90-day activity log lives on the device where you can read it. One button erases everything, on the device and on our servers.

Can it use my own AI account instead of yours?

Yes. Settings accepts an OpenAI, Google Gemini or Anthropic key, stored in the secure element. Health, money and family messages still route to PadClaw infrastructure unless you explicitly turn that protection off.

Does it need Wi-Fi?

Reminders, check-ins, alarms and voice control work offline. Channels that describe the outside world — weather, news, markets — need a connection, for the obvious reason.

Is it a real tablet too?

Yes. A full Android system sits one tap away for the family members who want a browser or a streaming app. It never interrupts the assistant, and the assistant never rearranges it.

Why only 3,000 units?

One line, one batch, every unit burned in for 24 hours and numbered. It's the largest quantity our team can hand-check and the smallest that pays for the tooling. Retail comes later, at $699.

What about repairs and parts?

The back comes off with Torx screws, the battery is a replaceable module, and we're stocking spare parts for five years. One-year warranty with a US return address.

Grandma speaks Mandarin. Her grandkids answer in English.

So does the demo — try the language toggle. English ships first; Mandarin and Spanish voice packs are the $650k stretch goal, and they're first in line either way.
