It's 9:02 AM. Your phone buzzes once.

"Mom finished her morning check-in ✓"

That's it. That's the product.

If you live an hour — or an ocean — away from your parents, you know the low hum of worry that never quite switches off. Did Dad take his blood-pressure pills? Is Mom eating breakfast? Should I call again, or is twice today already too much?

PadClaw turns that worry into one quiet green checkmark a day.

It's a 10-inch screen that lives in their living room. It greets them by name, reminds them gently, and when they tap "I'm OK today" — you know. No apps for them to learn. No passwords. No calls that start with "it's asking me to sign in again."

About this page: PadClaw is at the working-prototype stage (engineering validation complete, design validation underway). Product and lifestyle images on this page are AI-assisted concept visualizations of the intended experience, and screens shown represent planned software. Our campaign video demonstrates the current physical prototype. PadClaw is not a medical device or emergency-response system.

Three Steps. None of Them Are "Create an Account."

1 You set it up before gifting

Add their name, their reminders, and your phone number. Five minutes, done from your couch, before you wrap the box.

2 They plug it in and say hello

That's the entire setup on their end. One sentence. It already knows who they are and what matters.

3 You get the quiet note

A daily check-in confirmation to your phone. Missed check-ins nudge them first, then let you know.

The first — and only — setup screen they see. (Planned interface.)

Built for the Sandwich Generation

You're raising kids, holding down a career, and quietly managing your parents' wellbeing from another city. There are 50-plus million of you in America, and the tech industry keeps handing you two bad options:

  • Cameras and sensors that make their home feel like a security installation — and make them feel watched.
  • Apps and video calls that assume they'll remember passwords, update software, and troubleshoot Wi-Fi.

PadClaw is the third option: a screen they'll actually enjoy — their weather, their horoscope, photos of the grandkids, gentle reminders — that happens to keep you in the loop, with their knowledge and their dignity intact.

Dignity Is a Feature

  • No cameras pointed at anyone. Check-ins are something they do, not something done to them.
  • They see everything you see. The sharing screen shows exactly what goes to your phone — nothing is hidden from the person being "checked on."
  • No ads, no data resale. We sell hardware. Their medication schedule is not a marketing asset, and never will be.
  • One tap deletes everything — from the device and from our servers.

Risks and Challenges

Manufacturing. Tablets are a mature category and our ODM partner runs this exact class of device at scale today. The realistic risks are component lead times and certification queues; our schedule includes a one-quarter buffer.

AI service cost. The six-month Compute Pass is pre-funded from campaign proceeds and our GPU capacity is owned, not rented. Check-ins and reminders are engineered to run on-device precisely so the feature you bought this for can never be taken away.

Software scope. The check-in loop ships first and ships simple. If a stretch feature slips, the core promise is never delayed.

We publish a monthly update — with photos from the production line and the datacenter — from campaign close until the last unit ships.
